## 95. Internal Combustion Engines (the Four-Stroke Cycle) / Двигуни внутрішнього згоряння (чотиритактний цикл)

### EN — Short
Internal Combustion Engines (the Four-Stroke Cycle) describes a practical principle used to analyze, manufacture or maintain mechanical systems. It matters because it links measurable quantities to decisions about performance, reliability and safe design.

### EN — Detailed
- **Origin:** Nikolaus Otto developed a practical four-stroke gas engine in 1876, building on earlier engine work.
- **Core idea:** Intake, compression, power and exhaust take two crankshaft revolutions per complete cycle in a four-stroke cylinder.
- **Key components:** Define the input and output quantities, geometry, material behavior and applicable boundary conditions. Use consistent SI units: N for force, m for length, Pa for pressure or stress, N·m for torque and W for power when these quantities arise. The defining relation for this topic is reflected in the worked example; distinguish ideal predictions from measured behavior.
- **How to apply it:** Record the operating conditions and units before choosing a simplified model. Sketch the load path and identify the most critical interface, dimension or moving part. Calculate the first estimate, then compare it with a realistic allowable value or a measurable acceptance criterion. Repeat the check for the worst expected operating condition, including start-up, temperature variation and manufacturing scatter where applicable. A prototype test should reproduce the relevant load and should record both the predicted and observed result. If the discrepancy is substantial, investigate assumptions before increasing the rated capacity. For internal combustion engines (the four-stroke cycle), document which components are included and what constitutes successful performance.
- **Example:** A four-cylinder four-stroke engine runs at 3000 rpm. Each cylinder has 3000/2 = 1500 power strokes/min; total = 4 × 1500 = 6000 power strokes/min = 100/s. Check the unit conversion and interpret the numerical answer as an idealized engineering estimate.
- **Limitations:** The model does not automatically include tolerances, contamination, wear, thermal changes, fatigue or unexpected loading. Check the assumptions against real materials and supplier ratings, and avoid extrapolating a single calculation to every operating condition. Stop and isolate rotating equipment before inspection; guards and rated components are essential.
- **Related theories:** Power and Efficiency (#34); Rotational Dynamics (τ = I × α) (#38)
