## 99. Manufacturing Processes: Machining, Casting and 3D Printing / Виробничі процеси: механічна обробка, лиття та 3D-друк

### EN — Short
Manufacturing Processes: Machining, Casting and 3D Printing describes a practical principle used to analyze, manufacture or maintain mechanical systems. It matters because it links measurable quantities to decisions about performance, reliability and safe design.

### EN — Detailed
- **Origin:** Casting is ancient; powered machining grew during industrialization; additive manufacturing emerged commercially in the 1980s.
- **Core idea:** Machining removes material, casting solidifies it in a mold and 3D printing builds geometry layer by layer.
- **Key components:** Define the input and output quantities, geometry, material behavior and applicable boundary conditions. Use consistent SI units: N for force, m for length, Pa for pressure or stress, N·m for torque and W for power when these quantities arise. The defining relation for this topic is reflected in the worked example; distinguish ideal predictions from measured behavior.
- **How to apply it:** Record the operating conditions and units before choosing a simplified model. Sketch the load path and identify the most critical interface, dimension or moving part. Calculate the first estimate, then compare it with a realistic allowable value or a measurable acceptance criterion. Repeat the check for the worst expected operating condition, including start-up, temperature variation and manufacturing scatter where applicable. A prototype test should reproduce the relevant load and should record both the predicted and observed result. If the discrepancy is substantial, investigate assumptions before increasing the rated capacity. For manufacturing processes: machining, casting and 3d printing, document which components are included and what constitutes successful performance.
- **Example:** A 0.10 m × 0.05 m × 0.01 m aluminum block has V = 0.00005 m³. At 2700 kg/m³ its mass is 0.135 kg; removing 20% volume leaves 0.108 kg before process losses. Check the unit conversion and interpret the numerical answer as an idealized engineering estimate.
- **Limitations:** The model does not automatically include tolerances, contamination, wear, thermal changes, fatigue or unexpected loading. Check the assumptions against real materials and supplier ratings, and avoid extrapolating a single calculation to every operating condition. Verify critical dimensions and loads before fabricating a part that could injure people.
- **Related theories:** Material Classes: Metals, Polymers, Ceramics and Composites (#78); Tolerances and Fits (#82)
